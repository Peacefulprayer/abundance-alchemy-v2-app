What Meditation Is

Meditation is the practice of attention. It can involve stillness, breath, inward witnessing, presence, receptivity, or disciplined awareness.

Across traditions, meditation helps people return from fragmentation into steadiness.

Meditation is not escape from life. It is a return to presence within it.

Meditation Is More Than Silence

Attention

Meditation trains awareness.

Stillness

Meditation helps quiet internal noise.

Receptivity

Meditation creates room to notice what is already here.

Grounding

Meditation can regulate the body, calm the mind, and steady emotion.

Ways People Practice Meditation

Meditation appears in many forms across traditions and personal practice.

Breath meditation

Attention anchored in breathing.

Silent sitting

Stillness without forced output.

Guided meditation

Attention shaped by spoken support.

Contemplative meditation

Reflection held in inward quiet.

Mantra or phrase meditation

A repeated word or sacred phrase used as anchor.

Body awareness

Attention returning to sensation and embodied presence.

A Practice Found Across Traditions

Meditation appears in religious, spiritual, philosophical, and secular contexts. It may be devotional, contemplative, attentional, or therapeutic depending on the tradition and intention.

The purpose of this page is to honor that breadth without flattening the differences.

Meditation and Prayer

Meditation is often the practice of sacred attention. Prayer is one of the ways human beings speak toward the sacred.

For many people, the two belong together. One creates space to listen. The other creates space to speak.
